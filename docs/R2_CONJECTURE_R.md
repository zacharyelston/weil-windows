## M2: polynomial bounds [D]

The statement here concerns the exact analytic expressions underlying the certificates. It does not promise that a finite-precision program returns a ball of uniformly bounded radius at arbitrarily large inputs. On the supplied points G1 checks the actual stored upper endpoints separately.

Put `b=β=c−2`, `λ=√(c/(2π))` and `pref=√c b²`. For the registered range, `c≥4π` and `b>10`. Write `a_j(k)=(k+j)!/(2^j j!(k−j)!)`. All constants and the formal coefficients used below are recorded by the committed script in the registration and `conjecture_r_proof.json`; the manifest checks their transcription and exact finite inequalities.

### The pole coefficient: |a|≤10c

The real integral representation [DLMF 10.32.3](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.32.E3) [R] gives `0<I₂(b)≤I₀(b)`. Concavity of sine on the half interval gives `cos θ≤1−2θ²/π²` for `0≤θ≤π`. Enlarging the resulting Gaussian integral therefore gives

`I₂(b) ≤ exp(b) √π/(2√(2b)) < exp(b)/√b`.

The terminating half-integer formula [DLMF 10.49.8](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.49.E8) [R], after converting the spherical function, gives the following exact equalities [D]:

`I_(5/2)(b) = [exp(b)(1−3/b+3/b²) − exp(−b)(1+3/b+3/b²)]/√(2πb)`,

`I_(7/2)(b) = [exp(b)(1−6/b+15/b²−15/b³) + exp(−b)(1+6/b+15/b²+15/b³)]/√(2πb)`.

For `b≥10`, the positive-exponential polynomial in the latter is at least `2/5`, while the former polynomial is at most unity. Consequently

`I_(7/2)(b)≥(2/5)exp(b)/√(2πb)`, and `I_(5/2)(b)≤exp(b)/√(2πb)`.

In the sheet's notation `K_μ(0)=I_μ(b)/b^μ`. For either sign `cp`, the exact ζ constraint yields

`|a| ≤ I₂(b)/(pref K_(7/2)(0)) + K_(5/2)(0)/K_(7/2)(0)`

`≤ (5/2)b^(3/2)/λ + (5/2)b ≤ [(5/2)√(2π)+5/2]c <10c`.

This proves a uniform linear coefficient bound without a Bessel-ratio asymptotic. For non-pole objects the coefficient is zero. In particular the constraint denominator is positive in magnitude.

### Envelope integrals, including the cap split

For `ω≥c`,

`√(ω²−b²)≥ω√(4(c−1))/c≥ω/√c`.

Let `e=k+1+j`. For every actual base or derivative term, `e−p≥2`, `p≤4` and `2≤k≤5`. The weighted Hankel integral is bounded by

`√(2/π) Σ_j a_j(k) c^(e/2) ∫[c,∞) ω^(p−e)(1+log(ω/c)) dω`

`=√(2/π) Σ_j a_j(k)c^(p−e/2+1)[1/(e−p−1)+1/(e−p−1)²] ≤ 2·10000 c²`.

The last step uses the exact finite coefficient sums in the registration and `p−(k−1)/2≤2` for the actual term list. This already bounds the integral of the minimum envelope; however `env_G` may integrate a cap slightly past the crossover and hence exceed that minimum integral. To cover the implemented split construction as well, allow any `c≤ω*≤100c`. The cap is at most unity, and on its first interval the logarithmic weight is less than **6**. Hence its contribution is at most `6(100c)^5`. Adding the whole Hankel integral rather than only its remaining part gives the deliberately coarse bound

`G_(p,k) ≤ 7·10^10 c^5`.

The tail constructed with `C_E` at the final endpoint is also below the same whole-Hankel bound: its fixed square-root ratio is larger than the global ratio used above. This covers the exact cap/middle/tail expressions, without assuming envelope monotonicity.

The exact checks `hank_k(100)<cap_k` show the mathematical crossover is below that argument. Thus its frequency is at most `b+100`, which lies well inside the allowed frequency range for `c≥4π`; an explicitly fixed split such as `100c` is always available. The code's nearby floating split is an integration choice rather than a theorem input. The uniform analytic proof is stated with the bounded-split condition explicitly, instead of claiming a bound on arbitrary numerical rounding or quadrature overestimation.

The sums of term coefficient magnitudes are at most `1+2|a|` for the base envelope and `1+4|a|` for the derivative envelope. Both are bounded by **41c**. Therefore both analytic certificate integrals satisfy

`J₀,J₁≤3·10^12 c^6`.

### Edge sum and its remainder

For base terms, `p−e≤−3` and `p−(k+1)/2≤−½`. The same global Hankel bound gives

`E(nc) ≤ 10000(1+2|a|)c^(−½)n^(−3)`.

The finite edge sum and its `C_E` remainder are bounded by the same coefficient times the sum of the retained cubic powers plus their integral tail. This sum is at most **2**, independently of the truncation order. Since `√x pref/2≤√q c³/2`, the entire edge upper expression is at most

`√x|S(√x)|_upper ≤ 5·10^5 √q c^(7/2)`.

The pre-factor multiplying `J₀,J₁` is at most `√q c²`. Substituting the above estimates in the sheet's exact formulas gives

`B≤3·10^12 √q c^8`,

`A≤2(5·10^5 √q c^(7/2)) + (3·10^12/2)√q c^8 + 3·10^12 √q c^8 < C₁ c^8`.

Thus M2 holds for these analytic envelope majorants on the registered range. This is a polynomial existence bound; the constants intentionally sacrifice sharpness. The new upper-norm code and G1 use the existing certificates without altering `cert_lib.py`.

## What M1 would imply [D, conditional]

A safe count majorant, covering real and central zeros as well, is obtained directly from the read zero-count bounds:

`K_q=5(1+log(3q))`,

`N(t)≤K_q` below unit height, and `N(t)≤K_q t(1+log t)` above it.

For primitive Dirichlet characters, the bound at and above unit height follows by using `ℓ(t)≤log(3qt)`, `log(1+ℓ)≤ℓ`, and dropping the favorable constant terms in BMOR; its zero-free case only makes the bound easier. Monotonicity of the actual count extends its bound downward, with arbitrary multiplicity. For ζ, the same coarse majorant follows above the sheet's zero-free height from Bellotti–Wong and `log log t≤log t`; below that height use the sheet's standard zero-free input [S].

Choose the valid common tail constants `A=B=H=C₁c^8`. Stieltjes integration from their quotient cutoff gives

`Σ_ρ min(H²,H²/tρ²) ≤ 2H² K_q ∫[1,∞) (1+log t)t^(−2)dt = 4 K_q H²`.

This includes every low-height atom through the cumulative count. If M1 held with the registered constants, a corrected Theorem-2 certificate for the same KB trial would therefore satisfy

`B_R(x)≤4K_q C₁² C₂ c^(2p₁+p₂) exp(−2β) = C c^24 exp(−2c)`.

Here `B_R` names this safe choice of majorant; different overly loose choices of certificate constants need not obey the same bound. G3 separately checks the original stored certificate outputs against the registered target. The argument is conditional on M1 and proves no uniform statement about those outputs beyond the supplied points.

The registered exponent **24** is above the sheet's fitted range **3.45–11.5**. Those fits describe selected finite certificates, sometimes from a different β candidate; they do not determine a uniform exponent. The loose cap split explains part of this attempt's larger exponent. There is no claim here that the registered exponent is proved for Conjecture R.

## M1: unresolved quantitative step and stop report

The first missing step is an explicit error estimate for the **normalized, projected lattice sum**, uniformly in `c` and in a fixed positive `w` interval. Pointwise asymptotics of either summand are insufficient after their leading terms cancel, and do not by themselves justify summing the errors over the infinite character lattice.

There is additional cancellation from the ζ pole constraint. As a formal fixed-`y` calculation, put `r=2πy²`, `b=β`, and `a=0` first. Normalize both pieces by `exp(b−r/2)/√(2πb)`. Expanding the elementary arguments and the fixed-order Bessel expansion gives the first relative coefficients

`W₁(r)=−15/8+r/4−r²/8`,

`F₁(r)=−2+r/2−r²/8`,

so their difference is **`1/8−r/4`**. The `a` term adds `a(2r−1)` to this difference. The exact ζ minus constraint has formal limit **`a→1/8`**, which cancels this entire first coefficient, not just its value at the origin. These rational coefficients are reproduced in `conjecture_r_proof.json`. This calculation is a sketch of the cancellation mechanism, not a uniform asymptotic estimate; it supplies no numerical remainder threshold or M1 constant.

[DLMF §10.41(iv)](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.41#iv) [R] gives a fixed-order, large-argument expansion with an unspecified big-O term; its error discussion points to Olver. [DLMF §10.40](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.40#iii) [R] supplies scalar remainder machinery through the modified Bessel function of the second kind and continuation. This machinery could be used in a further derivation; it has not been converted here into the required summable error bound through the KB edge and pole projection. In particular the sheet's entire `K_ν` is not the DLMF modified second-kind `K_ν`, and its real interior argument approaches zero near the edge.

An eventual proof could choose a fixed interval inside the window, for example `[1,√2]`, obtain a nonzero limiting Hermite combination `h`, and prove a quantitative inequality of the form

`‖Σ χ(n)[ψ_c(nw/√q)/d_c−h(nw/√q)]‖_(L²(interval)) ≤ ε(c)`

with an explicit `ε(c)` smaller than a certified positive norm of the limiting sum. Here `d_c` is the first surviving exponential/power normalization. A nonzero polynomial Gaussian has a nonzero character theta sum: it is analytic on positive `w`, and its first lattice term dominates as `w→∞`. The identity theorem therefore gives qualitative positivity of its norm on the interval. This observation is [D], but supplies neither the requested certified quantitative constant nor the uniform KB remainder. No such constant has been computed in this attempt.

**Stop reason:** I could not source or establish the needed explicit uniform remainder for this normalized cancelled lattice sum. The brief requires stopping at that point. M1, the registered norm constant and the uniform rate have not been proved or disproved. This is an obstruction to completing the present argument from the estimates established here, not a counterexample to Conjecture R and not evidence of a different rate.

## Gate results [C, finite and conditional on the stored main integrals]

`conjecture_r_gates.json` records **G1: 92/92; G2: 92/92; G3: 92/92**, using only the specified candidates. No registered constant changed after a result was seen. All supplied inputs are eligible for the registered threshold.

G2 reconstructs the original exact dyadic endpoints at the original precision and checks that the stored printed endpoint balls contain them. For each band it uses the **upper** endpoint of the stored certified main-square integral and the larger of a freshly recomputed remainder bound and the stored remainder's upper endpoint. Its contribution is `(√I_upper+ε_upper√length)²`. It then adds `gap_length · S_sup²`, where `gap_length=upper(√x)−1−covered_length` and `S_sup` bounds the entire sum uniformly for positive `w` above unity. The global bound uses the compact kernel's supremum, its Fourier L¹ bound, finitely many terms, and a `C_E` cubic tail. Symmetry gives the full window norm. Thus rounded band gaps and the final endpoint sliver are covered upward.

This construction reuses the committed main-integral enclosures; it does not claim independent reproduction of the Arb integrals. The output records hashes of its inputs and `cert_lib.py` so that this dependency is explicit. A startup band-list assertion initially rejected the zero-length final band at square inputs; the script was corrected to match the original certificate's omission of zero-length bands. That was a bookkeeping error, not a failed mathematical gate, and no bound or target was relaxed.

G1 and G3 use a lower endpoint of the proposed upper majorant versus an upper endpoint of the stored quantity. G2 uses an upper endpoint of the proposed lower target versus the assembled actual-norm upper enclosure. Each test can reject its target; a successful G2 is only a necessary consistency test for a claimed lower bound. These deliberately slack targets passing finite tests do not resolve M1.
