### The Mellin correction is substantive [D]

Take the Fourier-invariant Gaussian `ψ(y)=exp(−πy²)` and the even primitive character of conductor **5**, with **δ=2**. These satisfy the stated class conditions. At **Im z=0.75**, the integral defining `Mψ` has exponent **−1.25** at the origin and diverges. This point is inside the claimed larger strip. Thus the ordinary-integral formulation is false there, even for a smooth trial.

For a bounded `ψ` near the origin, absolute Mellin convergence requires `Im z<½`; decay at infinity requires `Im z>−½−δ`. Fubini first proves the identity in the substrip where the Dirichlet series converges. Continuation then proves it throughout this smaller strip, which contains every nontrivial zero. The ζ pole is cancelled by the exact Fourier constraint as in the sheet. The zero-vanishing conclusion therefore survives.

Local bounded variation of each summand does not, on its own, justify bounded variation of an infinite locally uniformly convergent sum. A sufficient replacement is local uniform summability of the variations, for example a derivative bound giving uniform convergence of `Σ n χ(n) ψ′(nw/√q)` on compact positive `w` intervals. The actual Hermite and KB trials have the requisite derivative decay. This is a missing argument for the broad abstract class, not a constructed counterexample to bounded variation in that class.

### The zero-count correction [D from R]

[BMOR, Theorem 1.1](https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~andrewr/pubs/counting_zeros.pdf) [R] requires **T≥5/7**. Its zero-free conclusion additionally requires **ℓ≤1.567**. If the conductor is large enough that this second condition already fails at the smallest permitted height, taking the maximum with that height does not prove that lower heights are zero free. In particular it cannot silently discard real or central zeros.

Choose a reference height `T*` inside the published range with a valid nonnegative count bound. Define `N_ext(t)=N⁺(T*)` below `T*` and `N_ext(t)=N⁺(t)` above it. Monotonicity of the actual count justifies the constant extension; apply Stieltjes integration from the actual quotient cutoff using `N_ext`. An alternative safe, less sharp bound is

`Σ min(B²,A²/tρ²) ≤ B² N⁺(T*) + 2 A² ∫[T*,∞) N⁺(t)t⁻³ dt`.

Here the first term pays for all low zeros, with multiplicity; integration by parts bounds the high-zero contribution by the second term. No restriction on central multiplicity is needed. If the quotient cutoff is already above `T*`, the usual tail integral suffices without assuming a zero-free interval below it.

The stored Dirichlet trials have **0 range failures** when the lower endpoint of the quotient formed from the stored upper `A,B` endpoints is compared with the published height threshold. The code itself asserts that range. Thus this general-statement gap does not invalidate those finite cases.

There is also an endpoint issue: rounding a zero-free height upward does not extend the zero-free theorem. Use a downward enclosure for a zero-free endpoint, or an extended count between the two endpoints. For the stored cases whose quotient exceeds the count's validity threshold this endpoint issue is immaterial.

**Repair of Proposition H [D/C].** Define the analytic Hermite majorants `A_H=K_A λ^(d+1)exp(−πλ²)` and `B_H=K_B λ^(d−1)exp(−πλ²)`, with `K_B=2√q K_P Θ_(d−2)` and the sheet's `K_A`. Their quotient is `(K_A/K_B)λ²`, increasing with `λ`. The review script recomputes these constants as balls and verifies that the quotient's lower endpoint at the base window exceeds the upper endpoint of `t_low` for every tabulated Dirichlet sector: **0 uniform cutoff failures**. Thus the zero sum for these majorants starts inside the published range throughout the claimed parameter interval. Enlarging its integral downward to `t_low` is safe without asserting that smaller heights are zero free. This repairs the proof without changing its displayed constants.

## Audit of the certificate chain

**Norm lower bound [D/C].** On each exact dyadic subinterval the finite `S_main` is real on the real axis, so its square is nonnegative. The integral's lower endpoint bounds its squared norm downward. The omitted tail is uniformly bounded by `ε`; the reverse triangle inequality gives `‖S‖≥√I_lower−ε_upper√length`. The code checks positivity before squaring. The symmetry factor accounts for both window halves. Omitted endpoint gaps only make a lower bound smaller. The stored norm balls have **0 nonpositive certificates** in this audit.

For an upper bound, reverse the signs and use `I_upper`: `(√I_upper+ε_upper√length)²`. Then add a rigorous upper bound for the gaps, including the final endpoint sliver. The stored printed endpoint balls are not exact original endpoints; reconstruct the dyadic endpoints from the original input and precision. Reusing stored integral enclosures requires retaining exactly their original integration intervals.

**Envelopes [D/C].** `env_G` integrates a cap on its first interval and a Hankel envelope on its second, then bounds the remaining tail with a fixed lower ratio between the square root and its argument. The floating crossover is merely an exact dyadic split choice; neither formula needs that choice to be the true crossing. The analytic flags on the square root and logarithm let Arb reject branch-crossing quadrature boxes. `C_E` uses nonpositive remaining powers to turn every term into a uniform cubic-decay majorant. The same majorant bounds both the edge sum remainder and the per-band omitted `n` tail. Lemma H replaces an unproved monotonicity assertion with a valid nonnegative harmonic sum. The prefactors and the derivative terms in `A` have the stated directions.

Arb certification verifies the evaluated formula and its error accounting; it does not prove the mathematical formula's hypotheses. In particular this audit accepts the read zero-count theorems and the assumed explicit formula separately.

**Zero counts [R/S].** I recommend [Bellotti–Wong, arXiv v2, Theorem 1.1, second bound](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15470v2#Thmtheorem1.1): coefficients **0.11200, 0.12567, 3.77417**. This is a directly read replacement for the incomplete older proof and preserves the present certificate chain. Its low-height argument uses Platt's **|S(T)|≤2.5167**; that underlying computation remains [S] here. If a refereed-only policy is required, use HSW with **C₃=9.4925**, the correction explicitly stated in Bellotti–Wong's footnote, and regenerate the ζ certificates. I have not independently recalculated the sheet's quoted inflation factor.

The strip containing the nontrivial zeros is an unconditional Dirichlet/ζ fact, not a consequence solely of the displayed functional equation. The statement that no Euler product enters should be qualified: no Euler-product estimate is used in the bound after the standard zero-strip input has been supplied. No extension to functions with zeros outside that strip follows.

The §8(a) problem has an exact finite-dimensional illustration: take the positive diagonal form **diag(1,100)** and vector **(1,1)**. Its RQ is **50.5**, legitimately bounded by **60**. Its projection onto the second coordinate has RQ **100**, exceeding that bound. The manifest checks this example. A projected check can therefore fail even when the original-trial upper theorem is true. A captured fraction close to unity also does not control a quadratic-form error without a bound in the form norm.

For Table D, the reciprocal inequalities require a strictly positive minimum bracket and the same exact nonnegative numerator. On those hypotheses the ordering `RQ/Ritz≤RQ/λ≤RQ/cert` is correct. The table's floating ratios remain diagnostics. Upper bounds on the minimum make no inference about zeros lying on the critical line.
